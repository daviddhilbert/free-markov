## Readings fixed in Stage 3 (within the plan; no formal statement departs from the paper)

1. **Template diagrams.** Each Markov template is built from two anchored diagrams drawn
   with Stage 1's operations, and `α`, `β` match positions (`Template.ofDiagrams`); the
   letters `H, C, A`, `T` and the numerals of the drawings are exactly these position
   matchings. (4.1): `Ω_a` = `δ_a` glued to `δ_a ⊗ 1_a`, `Λ_a` = `δ_a` glued to
   `1_a ⊗ δ_a`. (4.2) as drawn discards the *second* prong: `Ω_a` = `δ_a` glued to
   `1_a ⊗ ε_a`, `Λ_a` the single edge; "similarly for right counitality" is the template
   with `ε_a ⊗ 1_a`. (4.3): `Ω_a` = `δ_a` glued to the node-free symmetry `σ_{aa}` (the
   duplicate with its outputs in the other order), `Λ_a` = `δ_a`. (4.4): `Ω_f` = the node
   `f` glued to a discard on each output, `Λ_f` = a discard on each letter of the source.
   Templates live in universe 0 (`Template.{0}`), as Stage 2 allows.
2. **Corollary 3.5 with one ordering.** `E_T` is generated by the template pairs for *all*
   compatible orderings; the paper applies Corollary 3.5 after checking the pairs as drawn.
   `SRelClosed.of_sRel` shows that one ordering per template suffices (the proof of
   `S_T ⊆ E_T` chooses the ordering of `Ω` freely), which is what the paper's argument
   uses. Stage 2's `cor_3_5` is unchanged.
3. **`δ_a`, `ε_a` on words** are the monoidal products of the comonoids on the letters
   (Mathlib's `ComonObj (A ⊗ B)`), i.e. defined through (1.2), which the paper states is
   "how `δ_a` and `ε_a` have been defined". The paper's description by a diagram `Γ`
   with a choice of prong order is `prongCopy l c` (the duplicates on the letters, each
   with its prongs in the order `c i`, joined through (1.2)); `prongCopy_eq` is the
   independence of the order. The identification of the paper's single diagram `Γ`
   (glued to `(⊗1) ⊗ (⊗1)` along a non-positional bijection) with this composite is the
   paper's own "represented by the same diagram" and was not formalized separately (open
   item 2 below).
4. **Proof methods differing from the paper** (statements unchanged): (1.1) for words is
   derived from the letters by Mathlib's theory of comonoids instead of `n` surgeries per
   law; (1.3) is proved by the generation principle (`J.induction`: (4.4) on generators,
   (1.2) for `⊗`, `σ_{1,1} = 1` for symmetries) instead of the paper's induction on inner
   nodes with a surgery at a maximal node. PLAN.md asked for the latter; see open item 1.
5. **Theorem 4.1's universes**: `IsFreeMarkov.{w, v, max w v, w, v', u'}`, so the targets
   `D : Type u'` with `Category.{v'} D` range over all universes.
6. **`F_s` on (4.4)** uses that the unit of a strict Markov category is terminal
   (`eq_discard`), equivalent to the paper's use of the second equality of (1.2) and (1.3)
   in `D`.
7. **Remark 4.2.** The symbolic language is `Syn_s(G)`. Duplicates and discards on its words
   are expressed through those on the letters by (1.2) (`copyS`, `discardS`). The
   congruence generated by the Markov axioms (`MarkovCong`) is stated on `Arrow Syn_s(G)`
   (no casts) and generated by (1.1) and (1.2) for all words and (1.3) for all morphisms.
   "Isomorphic quotients" is stated as: congruent iff equal images in `F_s(G)/S_M`
   (`remark_4_2`), and every morphism of `F_s(G)/S_M` is an image (`remark_4_2_surjective`);
   the quotient category of `Syn_s(G)` is not constructed. The coherence wording is
   `remark_4_2_semantic`: congruent iff equal under every interpretation in a strict Markov
   category matching duplicates and discards (targets in the universes of `F_s(G)/S_M` for
   the "if").

Steps the paper leaves to the reader that are proved here (PLAN.md, Section 6): (1.2) and
(1.1) for words ("this is how `δ_a`, `ε_a` have been defined", "`n` Markov surgeries");
independence of the prong order; that the pairs on which `F_s` agrees form a congruence
containing `S_M`; uniqueness of `F_m`.
